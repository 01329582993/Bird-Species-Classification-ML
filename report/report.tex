\begin{abstract}
Fine-grained visual categorization (FGVC) of bird species is a challenging
computer vision task owing to high intra-class variance and subtle
inter-class differences. This paper presents a systematic comparative study
of feature extraction paradigms for classifying a 20-species subset of the
Caltech-UCSD Birds-200-2011 (CUB-200-2011) dataset. We evaluate three
hand-crafted descriptor families---Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG),
Local Binary Patterns (LBP), and multi-colorspace Colour Histograms---both
individually and in combination, pairing each with five classical machine
learning classifiers (SVM, k-NN, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and Logistic
Regression). We then replace hand-crafted features with 512-dimensional deep
visual embeddings extracted from a frozen, pre-trained ResNet-18 backbone.
Our feature ablation reveals that classical descriptors plateau at
approximately 36\% test accuracy, while the hybrid deep-feature pipeline
achieves up to \textbf{79.55\%} accuracy with SVM---a gain of more than
40 percentage points---at a fraction of the computational cost of end-to-end
fine-tuning. We further analyse per-class confusion patterns to identify the
most and least discriminable species pairs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ================================================================
\IEEEPARstart{F}{ine-grained} visual categorization (FGVC) involves
distinguishing between visually similar sub-categories of a common class,
such as different bird species, car models, or aircraft types. Unlike
generic image classification, FGVC demands the capture of highly localised
and subtle discriminative cues---beak curvature, crown colour, wing-bar
patterns---while remaining robust to variation in pose, lighting, and
background clutter~\cite{wah2011cub}.

Bird species identification has practical applications in ecological
monitoring, biodiversity conservation, and citizen-science platforms such
as eBird. Automating this task at scale requires models that are both
accurate and computationally accessible. State-of-the-art FGVC methods
typically rely on end-to-end fine-tuned deep convolutional neural networks
augmented with attention mechanisms~\cite{lin2015bilinear,zhang2019part}.
However, such approaches demand large labelled datasets, powerful GPUs, and
substantial training time---resources often unavailable in
resource-constrained environments.

In this work, we study whether a \emph{hybrid} strategy---combining a
frozen pre-trained CNN as a static feature extractor with lightweight
classical classifiers---can close the accuracy gap relative to hand-crafted
descriptors, without incurring the cost of full network fine-tuning.

\noindent\textbf{Key Contributions:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A rigorous five-technique feature ablation study (HOG, LBP, Colour
        Histogram, Classical Combined, ResNet-18 Deep) evaluated under
        identical experimental conditions.
  \item A hybrid pipeline pairing frozen ResNet-18 deep embeddings with five
        tuned classical classifiers, achieving up to 79.55\% accuracy on a
        20-class CUB subset.
  \item Quantitative evidence that transfer-learned embeddings yield a
        $>$40 percentage-point accuracy gain over the best classical pipeline.
  \item A reproducible, publicly available implementation including
        data-preparation scripts and Google Colab notebooks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ================================================================

\subsection{Fine-Grained Visual Categorisation}
Fine-grained recognition has been extensively studied on benchmark datasets
including CUB-200-2011~\cite{wah2011cub}, Stanford
Cars~\cite{krause2013cars}, and FGVC-Aircraft~\cite{maji2013aircraft}. Early
approaches relied on part-based models and hand-annotated keypoints to focus
the classifier on discriminative regions~\cite{zhang2014part}. Bilinear
pooling methods such as the Bilinear CNN of Lin~\emph{et
al.}~\cite{lin2015bilinear} capture pairwise feature interactions and achieve
substantial accuracy on CUB. More recent transformer-based
approaches~\cite{he2021transfg} improve fine-grained discrimination through
token selection and self-attention but incur considerably higher computational
cost. Our work targets environments where such heavy models are impractical.

\subsection{Classical Feature Descriptors}
The Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG), introduced by Dalal and
Triggs~\cite{dalal2005hog}, captures local edge structure and has been widely
used for object detection and recognition. Local Binary Patterns
(LBP)~\cite{ojala2002lbp} encode micro-texture by thresholding pixel
neighbourhoods and are effective for texture classification. Colour
histograms in multiple perceptual colour spaces (HSV, CIELAB) provide
complementary chromatic descriptors~\cite{swain1991color}. While these
hand-crafted features are fast and interpretable, their discriminative power
is inherently limited for fine-grained categories where inter-class
differences are subtle.

\subsection{Transfer Learning for Image Classification}
He~\emph{et al.}~\cite{he2016resnet} demonstrated that deep residual
networks pre-trained on ImageNet learn generic, transferable visual
representations. Several works have shown that using a frozen pre-trained
CNN as a feature extractor, combined with a lightweight SVM or linear
classifier, yields competitive accuracy without the cost of full
fine-tuning~\cite{sharif2014cnn,donahue2014decaf}. Our work applies this
strategy to fine-grained bird classification and systematically compares it
against multiple classical descriptor baselines.

\section{Dataset and Preprocessing Pipeline}
\label{sec:data}
% ================================================================
The CUB-200-2011 dataset~\cite{wah2011cub} contains 11,788 images spanning
200 bird species. We target a 20-species subset selected by matching class
folder names against a curated list of family keywords (e.g.,
\textit{Crow}, \textit{Blackbird}, \textit{Grebe}, \textit{Gull}), yielding
approximately 1,170 images. We apply a stratified 70/15/15\% split for
training, validation, and testing respectively.

\subsection{Bounding Box Cropping}
Initial experiments showed very low accuracy ($\approx$11--17\%),
attributable to background clutter dominating the descriptors. We resolved
this by cropping each image to the ground-truth bounding box from
\texttt{bounding\_boxes.txt} before resizing, concentrating all descriptors
on avian features.

\subsection{Feature Standardisation}
Different descriptor families have vastly different numerical scales. We
apply \texttt{StandardScaler} (zero mean, unit variance) to the feature
matrix before model training, preventing high-magnitude dimensions from
dominating distance-based classifiers.

\section{Feature Extraction Methodologies}
\label{sec:features}
% ================================================================
We explore two paradigms: (i)~hand-crafted descriptors and
(ii)~transfer-learned deep embeddings.

\subsection{Hand-Crafted Feature Descriptors}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{HOG}~\cite{dalal2005hog}: Multi-channel HOG (grayscale,
        R, G, B), extended to a spatial pyramid ($1{\times}1$ +
        $2{\times}2$), producing an 8,100-dimensional descriptor.

  \item \textbf{LBP}~\cite{ojala2002lbp}: Multi-scale LBP combining fine
        ($R{=}1, P{=}8$) and coarse ($R{=}3, P{=}24$) texture, yielding
        a 10-dimensional histogram.

  \item \textbf{Multi-Colorspace Colour Histograms}~\cite{swain1991color}:
        32-bin histograms concatenated from RGB, HSV, and CIELAB spaces,
        producing a 96-dimensional descriptor.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deep Visual Embeddings}
We load ResNet-18~\cite{he2016resnet} pre-trained on ImageNet, freeze all
parameters, and replace the final classification head with an
\texttt{Identity} module, producing a 512-dimensional embedding per image.

% ================================================================
\section{Classification and Hyperparameter Tuning}
\label{sec:classifiers}
% ================================================================
We evaluate five classifiers on every feature representation, with
hyperparameters selected by grid search on the validation set:
(1)~\textbf{SVM}: RBF kernel, $C \in \{1,10,50,100\}$;
(2)~\textbf{k-NN}: $k \in \{3,5,7,11\}$, distance-weighted;
(3)~\textbf{Decision Tree}: entropy/gini, depth $\in \{5,10,20,\infty\}$;
(4)~\textbf{Random Forest}: 100--500 estimators;
(5)~\textbf{Logistic Regression}: L2, $C \in \{0.1,1,10\}$, one-vs-rest.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ================================================================

\subsection{Why Classical Descriptors Plateau}
Despite progressive engineering---bounding box cropping, spatial pyramids,
multi-scale LBP, and multi-colorspace histograms---the best classical
pipeline plateaus at $\approx$36\% accuracy. Two fundamental limitations
explain this. First, HOG and LBP encode local image statistics and cannot
capture global semantic relationships (e.g., relative beak-to-body
proportions) necessary for fine-grained discrimination. Second, the
8,206-dimensional classical feature space is sparse relative to the
$\approx$820 training images, inducing the curse of dimensionality that
degrades all classifiers.

\subsection{Why Deep Embeddings Succeed}
ResNet-18, pre-trained on 1.2 million ImageNet images, has learned
hierarchical visual abstractions that transfer well to bird morphology. The
512-dimensional embedding is dense and semantically meaningful: intra-class
variance is compressed and inter-class separability is maximised, explaining
why a simple linear SVM on these features substantially outperforms the
richest classical pipeline.

\subsection{Classifier Comparison}
SVM consistently performs best on both feature types, reflecting its strong
generalisation via margin maximisation. Logistic Regression performs
competitively on deep features, suggesting near-linear separability in the
ResNet-18 embedding space. Decision Tree performs worst due to overfitting on
the small training set.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Results are on a 20-class subset; performance on the full 200-class
        CUB dataset warrants future investigation.
  \item No data augmentation was applied; augmentation is expected to improve
        generalisation, particularly for classical features.
  \item The ResNet-18 backbone is frozen; end-to-end fine-tuning would likely
        further increase accuracy.
  \item The system has not been evaluated on out-of-distribution images
        (occluded birds, unseen lighting conditions).
\end{itemize}

% ================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ================================================================
We presented a systematic comparative study of five feature extraction
techniques paired with five classical classifiers for fine-grained bird
species classification on a 20-class CUB-200-2011 subset. Our feature
ablation study demonstrates that hand-crafted descriptors (HOG, LBP, Colour
Histograms) are fundamentally limited for fine-grained recognition,
plateauing at 36.36\% accuracy regardless of engineering effort. Replacing
hand-crafted features with 512-dimensional embeddings from a frozen
ResNet-18 backbone yields 79.55\% test accuracy with SVM---a 43-point
improvement obtained without any gradient update. This confirms that
transfer-learned deep representations are highly effective even when the
backbone is kept frozen, making the approach practical for
resource-constrained environments.

Future work will explore end-to-end backbone fine-tuning, data augmentation
strategies, attention-based part localisation, and extension to the full
200-class CUB dataset.
